# Core Resources in Magic: The Gathering

The three fundamental resources that determine game outcomes.

## Card Advantage

### Eric Taylor's Definition

Card advantage is the difference in cards available between players. The player with more options has more paths to victory.

**Simple card advantage**: Drawing extra cards, 2-for-1 trades
**Complex card advantage**: Board states where your cards matter more

### Actual vs Virtual Card Advantage

**Actual card advantage**: Literal card count difference
- Divination: +1 card
- Wrath of God killing 3 creatures: +2 cards
- Mulliganing: -1 card

**Virtual card advantage**: Cards that "don't count"
- Dead cards in hand (removal vs creatureless deck)
- Excess lands when flooded
- Expensive spells you'll never cast
- Narrow answers with no targets

A 2-for-1 against virtual cards isn't real advantage. Killing two Grizzly Bears with Wrath when opponent is hellbent is worse than just developing your board.

### Card Parity and Breaking It

Games start at parity (7 cards each, alternating draws). Advantages come from:
- Drawing more cards
- Trading up (1 card removes 2)
- Making opponent's cards virtual
- Generating card-like effects (tokens, recursion)

### Why Card Advantage Compounds

Early card advantage leads to more card advantage:
1. More cards = more options
2. More options = better play selection
3. Better plays = more favorable trades
4. Favorable trades = more cards

This is why early-game card draw (Preordain) is often stronger than late-game (Opportunity).

## Tempo

### EDT's Framework

Tempo is the rate at which you convert mana into board presence. Mana is time; each turn you get one land drop worth of development.

**Tempo-positive**: Your mana investment exceeds theirs
- Spell Pierce on a 4-drop: 1 mana answers 4 mana
- Unsummon on a creature they paid to cast + equip

**Tempo-negative**: Your mana investment loses to theirs
- Day of Judgment when you have more creatures
- Counterspell on a 1-drop

### Tempo vs Card Advantage Tradeoff

Many cards trade tempo for cards or vice versa:

| Card | Tempo | Cards |
|------|-------|-------|
| Divination | -3 mana (tempo loss) | +1 card |
| Unsummon | +tempo (mana differential) | -1 card (goes to hand) |
| Man-o'-War | Tempo-positive | Card-neutral |
| Mulldrifter | Tempo-negative | Card-positive |

### When Tempo Matters Most

Tempo dominates when:
- Games are short (aggro vs aggro)
- Board presence determines combat
- Life totals are pressured
- Neither player has inevitability

Tempo matters less when:
- Games go long
- Card quality trumps speed
- One player has clear inevitability

### The Tempo Spectrum

**Pure tempo plays**: Mana efficiency, no card investment
- Delver of Secrets (1 mana, 3 power)
- Daze (0 mana interaction)

**Balanced plays**: Fair mana-to-effect ratio
- Tarmogoyf (2 mana, 4/5)
- Lightning Bolt (1 mana, 3 damage)

**Card advantage plays**: Mana-inefficient but card-positive
- Fact or Fiction (5 mana, ~2 cards)
- Cryptic Command (4 mana, 2 effects)

## The Philosophy of Fire

### The Shock Baseline

From Sligh/Red Deck Wins theory: damage has a conversion rate to cards.

**Shock = 2 damage for 1 card and 1 mana**

This establishes baselines:
- 20 life = 10 Shocks = 10 cards of damage
- But you start with 7 cards
- Therefore: need ~3 damage per card average to win

### Life-to-Cards Conversion

Aggressive decks treat life total as a resource that converts to cards:

| Life Paid | Cards "Gained" |
|-----------|----------------|
| 2 life (Gitaxian Probe) | 1 card |
| 2 life (shock land untapped) | ~0.5 tempo |
| 4 life (Dismember) | Removal flexibility |

The Philosophy of Fire says: **damage IS a resource** that eventually converts to game win.

### Why the Last Life Point Matters

"The only life point that matters is the last one."

Paying 19 life for advantage is fine if you win before dying. This enables:
- Aggressive mulliganing for aggro
- Fetchland/shockland manabases
- Phyrexian mana
- Necropotence-style effects

### Reach and Inevitability

Pure Philosophy of Fire decks need **reach**: damage that ignores blockers.
- Burn spells
- Haste creatures
- Evasion

Without reach, board stalls favor the player with more cards (not more damage dealt).

## Board Presence (The Limited Resource)

### Board State as Primary Resource

In Limited specifically, board presence often matters more than hand size:
- Creatures trade in combat
- Board complexity creates virtual advantage
- Empty boards favor card draw; full boards favor board impact

### CABS Theory Application

Cards Affecting Board State (CABS) matters because:
1. Limited is creature-combat format
2. Card draw without board impact falls behind
3. Removal is premium because it affects board

### When Board Presence Beats Cards

The player with better board position can:
- Attack profitably
- Force bad blocks
- Pressure planeswalkers
- Create racing situations

Card advantage only helps if you survive to use those cards.

## Resource Interactions

### Trading Resources

Players constantly trade resources:
- Life for cards (Dark Confidant)
- Cards for tempo (Force of Will)
- Tempo for life (not attacking to hold blockers)
- Cards for board (casting spells)

### Resource Conversion Efficiency

Better players convert resources more efficiently:
- Knowing when life is expendable
- Identifying virtual cards
- Timing tempo pushes
- Sequencing for maximum value

### Format Context for Resource Priority

| Format Speed | Primary Resource |
|--------------|------------------|
| Hyper-aggro | Tempo > Life > Cards |
| Aggressive | Tempo ≈ Life > Cards |
| Midrange | Board > Cards > Tempo |
| Control | Cards > Board > Tempo |
| Combo | Specific cards > All else |

Understanding resource priority helps evaluate cards correctly for each format.
