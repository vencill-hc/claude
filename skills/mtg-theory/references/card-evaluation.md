# Card Evaluation Methods

Systematic approaches to evaluating cards for Limited, Constructed, and Cube.

## Quadrant Theory

### Marshall Sutcliffe's Framework

Evaluate cards across four board states (quadrants):

### Quadrant 1: Developing

**Board state:** Early game, both players establishing position
**What matters:** Mana efficiency, curve considerations, setup

**Good developing cards:**
- Cheap creatures (Elvish Mystic, Savannah Lions)
- Ramp (Rampant Growth, signets)
- Setup (Preordain, cantrips)

**Bad developing cards:**
- Expensive finishers (stuck in hand)
- Reactive cards (nothing to react to)
- Win-more effects

**Example:** Grizzly Bears is great developing, terrible everywhere else.

### Quadrant 2: Parity

**Board state:** Neither player ahead, board is stalled or even
**What matters:** Breaking through, gaining advantage

**Good parity cards:**
- Card draw (breaks stall with options)
- Evasion (gets damage through)
- Removal (clears path)
- Card quality (finds what you need)

**Bad parity cards:**
- Vanilla creatures (get stalled)
- Auras (risky, no immediate impact)
- Situational answers

**Example:** Mulldrifter is excellent at parity—develops AND draws cards.

### Quadrant 3: Ahead

**Board state:** You're winning, need to close the game
**What matters:** Speed, protection, inevitability

**Good ahead cards:**
- Haste creatures (immediate impact)
- Protection (shields your advantage)
- Reach (ends game from nowhere)
- Evasion (pushes through)

**Bad ahead cards:**
- Card draw (winning already, don't need cards)
- Defensive cards (not under pressure)
- Slow threats (give opponent time)

**Example:** Glorybringer is premium when ahead—haste + removal.

### Quadrant 4: Behind

**Board state:** You're losing, need to catch up
**What matters:** Stabilization, comeback potential

**Good behind cards:**
- Wraths (reset the board)
- Lifegain with impact (Timely Reinforcements)
- Walls/blockers (buy time)
- Removal (answer key threats)

**Bad behind cards:**
- Creatures without immediate impact
- Card draw (need board presence, not cards)
- Win-more effects

**Example:** Wrath of God is best when behind—equalizes immediately.

### Quadrant Scoring

Rate cards 1-5 in each quadrant:

| Card | Dev | Par | Ahd | Bhd | Total |
|------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-------|
| Grizzly Bears | 4 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 9 |
| Mulldrifter | 2 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 13 |
| Wrath of God | 1 | 3 | 1 | 5 | 10 |
| Glorybringer | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 15 |

**Bombs score high in multiple quadrants.** That's what makes them bombs.

## BREAD

### The Classic Limited Heuristic

**B**ombs > **R**emoval > **E**vasion > **A**ggro (Abilities) > **D**uds

### Bombs
Cards that win the game if unanswered. Pack 1 Pick 1 material.
- Planeswalkers
- Powerful rares/mythics
- Cards requiring immediate answer

### Removal
Answers to threats. Premium in creature-focused formats.
- Unconditional removal (Murder, Doom Blade)
- Conditional removal (Shock, Pacifism)
- Combat tricks (context-dependent)

### Evasion
Creatures that get damage through.
- Flying
- Menace
- Unblockable
- Trample (on large creatures)

### Aggro/Abilities
Creatures that impact the board.
- Good stats for cost
- Relevant abilities
- Synergy pieces

### Duds
Cards that don't affect the board meaningfully.
- Bad vanilla creatures
- Narrow situational cards
- Overcosted effects

### BREAD Limitations

BREAD is a starting point, not gospel:
- Ignores format speed (fast formats devalue bombs)
- Ignores synergy (linear decks want specific pieces)
- Ignores curve (need 2-drops even if "less powerful")
- Treats removal as fungible (it's not)

## CABS Theory

### Cards Affecting Board State

In Limited, prioritize cards that change the board position.

**High CABS:**
- Creatures (especially with ETB)
- Removal
- Combat tricks (context)
- Token generators

**Low CABS:**
- Pure card draw
- Lifegain without board impact
- Mill (usually)
- Auras (high variance)

### Why CABS Matters

Limited is fundamentally a creature combat format:
1. Creatures deal damage
2. Creatures block damage
3. Board position determines combat outcomes
4. Cards in hand don't block

### CABS in Different Formats

**Aggressive Limited format:** CABS is king
**Grindy Limited format:** Card advantage matters more
**Cube:** Depends on power level and archetype

### CABS Exceptions

- Control decks value card draw
- Combo decks value specific pieces
- Some formats reward non-CABS strategies

## The Dialogue Framework

### Questions and Answers

Every card is either a **question** (threat) or an **answer** (response):

**Questions:**
- Creatures
- Planeswalkers
- Enchantments that must be dealt with
- Win conditions

**Answers:**
- Removal
- Counterspells
- Discard
- Interaction

### Temporal Mismatch

The relationship between question cost and answer cost defines format speed:

**Cheap questions + expensive answers = Aggro format**
- 1-mana threats require 3+ mana to answer
- Board develops faster than removal
- Example: Delver of Secrets vs Doom Blade

**Expensive questions + cheap answers = Control format**
- Threats cost 5+, answers cost 1-2
- Removal is efficient
- Example: Titan vs Path to Exile

### Dialogue in Limited

Limited sets are designed with dialogue in mind:
- Common removal answers common threats
- Uncommon removal answers uncommon threats
- Rares often require rare/mythic answers

**Broken dialogues:**
- Threats with no common answers
- Removal too efficient for threats
- Hexproof/protection breaking interaction

### Format Health

Healthy formats have balanced dialogue:
- Threats and answers at comparable costs
- Multiple answer types available
- Threats not completely unanswerable

## Format-Specific Examples

### Return to Ravnica Limited

**Format characteristics:**
- Multicolor focus
- Synergy-driven
- Medium speed

**Evaluation adjustments:**
- Fixing is premium
- Gold cards more playable
- Guild synergies matter

**Stab Wound analysis:**
- 3 mana removal + clock
- Excellent in slow/grindy matchups
- Weak vs aggro (too slow)
- Format allowed it to shine

### Khans of Tarkir Limited

**Format characteristics:**
- Three-color focus
- Morph mechanic
- Slower format

**Evaluation adjustments:**
- Fixing extremely premium
- 2/2 for 3 (morphs) is baseline
- Expensive cards playable

### Modern Horizons Cube

**Format characteristics:**
- High power level
- Synergy matters
- Fast and interactive

**Evaluation adjustments:**
- Efficiency matters
- Linear strategies viable
- GRBS tolerance higher

## Ceiling vs Floor Analysis

### The Ceiling

Best-case scenario for a card:
- Perfect board state
- Ideal supporting cards
- Everything goes right

### The Floor

Worst-case scenario:
- Wrong matchup
- Bad board state
- No support

### Evaluation Balance

**High ceiling, low floor:** Risky, high variance
- Auras
- Combo pieces
- Narrow answers

**High ceiling, high floor:** Premium
- Efficient threats
- Versatile removal
- Modal cards

**Low ceiling, high floor:** Role players
- Vanilla creatures
- Basic effects
- Consistent but unexciting

**Low ceiling, low floor:** Unplayable
- Overcosted effects
- Narrow and weak
- No good scenarios

### Floor Matters More in Competitive Play

Consistency beats explosiveness over many games. High-floor cards:
- Reduce variance
- Provide reliable game plans
- Don't require specific conditions

## Common Evaluation Mistakes

### 1. Best-Case Thinking
"This card could..." Focus instead on what it usually does.

### 2. Ignoring Format Speed
A 6-mana bomb is unplayable if games end turn 5.

### 3. Comparison to Wrong Format
Standard cards aren't evaluated for Modern. Limited cards aren't evaluated for Constructed.

### 4. Ignoring Opportunity Cost
Every card in your deck displaces another card. Is this better than alternatives?

### 5. Synergy Without Support
Build-around cards need the build-around present.

### 6. Results-Oriented Thinking
"I won with this card" ≠ "This card is good." Sample size matters.
